# Diagnosis Loop

Use this file before choosing an intervention. The goal is to convert messy operating symptoms into a clear constraint, not to showcase frameworks.

## Operating Diagnosis

1. Define the outcome that is under pressure: speed, quality, margin, cash, growth, retention, reliability, morale, or customer trust.
2. Locate the system boundary: which team, process, customer segment, cost center, or decision loop is involved.
3. Separate symptom from mechanism:
   - Symptom: visible pain such as delays, churn, cost spikes, or missed targets.
   - Mechanism: the repeatable reason the pain keeps happening.
4. Identify the active constraint:
   - Process constraint: work queues, unclear handoffs, manual rework, or missing standards.
   - Economic constraint: weak margin, bad payback, cash timing, or low productivity per dollar.
   - Organization constraint: unclear ownership, missing capability, overloaded leaders, or weak accountability.
   - Customer constraint: poor segment fit, expectation mismatch, bad onboarding, or service failure.
   - Data constraint: decisions made from anecdotes, lagging metrics, or inconsistent definitions.
   - Rhythm constraint: goals, reviews, meetings, and decisions do not create a closed loop.
5. Choose the narrowest intervention that changes the mechanism.

## Stage Fit

- 0-1 validation: favor customer learning, fast manual operations, and explicit assumptions. Do not overbuild process.
- 1-10 repeatability: document the winning path, remove obvious handoff waste, and define basic metrics and owners.
- 10-100 scaling: standardize core flows, build management cadence, automate repeatable work, and clarify decision rights.
- Mature optimization: refine unit economics, reliability, talent systems, customer segmentation, and portfolio tradeoffs.

## Methodology Internalization

Use methods as hidden lenses:

- Bottleneck thinking asks where one constraint limits the whole system.
- Lean thinking asks which steps do not create customer or business value.
- Root-cause thinking asks why the same failure repeats.
- Goal-system thinking asks whether priorities, metrics, and review cadence reinforce each other.
- Unit-economics thinking asks whether growth converts into durable profit and cash.
- Customer-intelligence thinking asks which segment is affected, at what journey point, and why.

Only name a method if the user needs a shared format. Otherwise translate it into direct operating actions.

## Diagnostic Questions

Ask or infer only what matters:

- What changed recently: volume, team size, customer mix, pricing, channel, product complexity, or process ownership?
- Which metric moved first, and which moved second?
- Where does work wait, get redone, or require escalation?
- Which cost grows faster than revenue or value delivered?
- Which decisions require the founder or senior leader too often?
- Which customer segment is most valuable, most unhappy, or most likely to churn?
- What evidence would disprove the current explanation?

## Decision Rules

- If volume grew and cycle time rose, inspect handoffs and queues before hiring.
- If cost rose while growth quality fell, inspect channel/customer mix before cutting evenly.
- If execution slowed after hiring, inspect role clarity and management rhythm before adding tools.
- If churn rose, segment by customer value and lifecycle stage before changing the whole product or service.
- If every issue escalates upward, clarify decision rights and thresholds before adding meetings.
- If metrics are disputed, define metric ownership and source of truth before optimizing.

## Anti-Patterns

- Recommending a model without saying what behavior changes.
- Solving all domains at once.
- Cutting costs evenly across functions.
- Automating a broken process before removing unnecessary steps.
- Adding headcount to compensate for unclear priorities.
- Treating all customers as equally valuable or equally at risk.
- Measuring only lagging outcomes and missing early warning indicators.
