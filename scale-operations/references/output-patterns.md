# Output Patterns

Use these structures to keep recommendations executable. Choose the smallest format that fits the user's request.

## Operating Diagnosis Memo

Use for ambiguous or cross-functional problems.

Structure:

1. Situation: business context, stage, symptom, and time horizon.
2. Diagnosis: likely constraint, supporting evidence, and assumptions.
3. Priority moves: 3-5 actions ordered by leverage.
4. Ownership: accountable owner, collaborators, and decision rights.
5. Metrics: leading, lagging, quality, and countermetrics.
6. Cadence: review frequency, meeting or dashboard, and decision rule.
7. Risks: failure signals and backup moves.

## 30/60/90 Operating Plan

Use for broad scaling work.

- First 30 days: stabilize, measure, and remove obvious ambiguity.
- Days 31-60: standardize the winning flow and assign owners.
- Days 61-90: automate, hire, or redesign only after evidence supports it.

Each phase needs an outcome, actions, owner, metrics, and stop-doing list.

## Workflow Redesign Plan

Use for process problems.

- Process boundary.
- Current-state assumptions.
- Bottleneck or rework node.
- Proposed future state.
- SOP or decision-right changes.
- Automation candidates.
- Metrics and quality gates.
- Rollout plan.

## Cost-Control Plan

Use for financial pressure.

- Economic diagnosis.
- Costs to protect.
- Costs to stop, renegotiate, reduce, or redesign.
- Segment or unit-economics view.
- Cash and budget cadence.
- Owner by cost line.
- Countermetrics to prevent damage.

## Team Execution Plan

Use for hiring, leadership, and execution problems.

- Execution gap.
- Role and ownership changes.
- Management cadence.
- Hiring or capability plan.
- Performance and feedback loop.
- Metrics and decision latency checks.

## Customer Retention Plan

Use for churn, satisfaction, loyalty, or CRM problems.

- Segment at risk.
- Journey point where value breaks.
- Root-cause hypotheses.
- Recovery actions.
- Product/process/sales feedback loop.
- Metrics and early warning signals.

## Language Rules

- Prefer verbs and operating nouns over model names.
- State tradeoffs and sequencing.
- Make missing data explicit without stalling.
- Use tables only when they improve execution clarity.
- End with the next concrete review or decision point, not a generic offer.
