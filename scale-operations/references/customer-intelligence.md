# Customer Intelligence

Use this file for customer relationship management, customer data analysis, churn, retention, satisfaction, service quality, and loyalty.

## Customer Diagnosis

Do not treat the customer base as one average. Segment first:

- Value: revenue, margin, strategic value, referral value.
- Lifecycle: trial, onboarding, activation, repeat, renewal, expansion, winback.
- Behavior: usage, purchase frequency, support load, engagement, complaint pattern.
- Source: channel, campaign, geography, salesperson, partner, or cohort.
- Need: job-to-be-done, use case, industry, company size, or service tier.

Then locate where the relationship breaks: expectation, onboarding, value realization, service reliability, pricing, communication, or renewal process.

## Retention and Churn Logic

Diagnose churn by asking:

- Which segment changed?
- At what lifecycle point do they drop?
- What leading behavior changed before churn?
- Which promise did they believe they were buying?
- Which experience failed to deliver that promise?
- Which customers are worth saving, expanding, or letting go?

Avoid generic retention campaigns until the failing segment and journey point are clear.

## Translating Customer Models Into Actions

- Funnel thinking becomes: identify the stage with the biggest qualified drop-off and fix that step first.
- Cohort thinking becomes: compare customers who started at different times to avoid hiding recent deterioration.
- Recency/frequency/value thinking becomes: choose who gets personal outreach, automation, discounting, or no action.
- Satisfaction scoring becomes: trigger recovery actions and identify root causes, not just collect scores.
- Voice-of-customer work becomes: convert repeated complaints into product, process, service, or expectation-setting changes.
- Customer health scoring becomes: combine behavior, value, support risk, and relationship signals into an action queue.

## Customer Operating System

Build a weekly or monthly loop:

1. Review segment-level retention, activation, support, and satisfaction signals.
2. Identify the highest-value at-risk segment.
3. Assign owner for root-cause investigation and recovery actions.
4. Run targeted experiments, not broad campaigns.
5. Feed repeated causes into product, operations, sales, or onboarding changes.
6. Track whether the intervention changes leading behavior before lagging retention moves.

## Metrics

- Activation rate.
- Time to first value.
- Repeat purchase or usage frequency.
- Gross and net revenue retention.
- Logo retention.
- Churn by segment and cohort.
- Expansion rate.
- NPS, CSAT, CES, and complaint rate.
- First response time and resolution time.
- Support cost by segment.
- Health score movement.

Use countermetrics such as discount rate, support burden, and margin to avoid saving unprofitable relationships at any cost.

## Failure Signals

- Team celebrates average satisfaction while high-value customers decline.
- Churn analysis ignores customer start date or acquisition channel.
- Support works harder but product or process root causes remain unchanged.
- Sales promises create onboarding failures.
- Retention campaigns target everyone with the same message.
- Loyalty programs discount customers who would have stayed anyway.
