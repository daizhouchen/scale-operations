# Economic Control

Use this file for cost control, budgets, unit economics, cash flow, margin, payback, and resource allocation.

## Economic Diagnosis

Avoid blunt cost cutting. First decide which economic problem exists:

- Margin problem: revenue is not converting into contribution profit.
- Cash timing problem: collections, inventory, payroll, or vendor timing creates pressure.
- Growth quality problem: acquisition or expansion spend brings weak customers.
- Productivity problem: headcount or tools grow faster than output.
- Complexity problem: too many products, segments, channels, or custom requests dilute margin.
- Governance problem: budgets exist but owners lack decision rights or variance review.

## Cost-Control Ladder

1. Protect spend that creates near-term revenue, retention, or required reliability.
2. Stop spend with no owner, no metric, or stale assumptions.
3. Renegotiate large recurring vendors and remove unused seats.
4. Reduce process waste before reducing service quality.
5. Segment customers/products by profitability and support burden.
6. Shift work from custom/manual to standard/repeatable.
7. Change pricing, packaging, minimums, or service levels if the economics are structurally wrong.

## Unit Economics Translation

Use unit economics to answer operating questions:

- Are we buying customers we can profitably serve?
- Which segment pays back fastest?
- Which channel creates support or churn burden later?
- Which product or service tier consumes hidden labor?
- Which operational cost scales with volume, and which should stay fixed?

Do not average away reality. Segment by customer type, channel, product, geography, or cohort when decisions differ.

## Budget and Cash Rhythm

Create a monthly control loop:

1. Forecast revenue, gross margin, payroll, discretionary spend, collections, and cash runway.
2. Assign budget owners for controllable lines.
3. Review budget versus actuals and explain variance by driver.
4. Decide corrective actions before the next month closes.
5. Track commitments, not only paid invoices.

For cash pressure, move from monthly to weekly cash review until stable.

## Metrics

- Gross margin and contribution margin.
- Revenue per employee.
- Cost per order, ticket, project, or account.
- CAC, payback period, and LTV/CAC where applicable.
- Burn multiple and runway for startups.
- Budget variance by owner.
- Vendor spend utilization.
- Support cost by segment.
- Discount rate and gross revenue retention.

Use countermetrics such as churn, quality, uptime, employee load, and sales velocity to avoid destructive cuts.

## Failure Signals

- Across-the-board cuts hit growth-critical work.
- Cost owners cannot explain variance.
- CAC looks acceptable in aggregate but weak in specific channels.
- High-revenue customers consume unpriced service work.
- Hiring plans are disconnected from throughput or revenue capacity.
- Tool consolidation saves money but increases manual labor elsewhere.
